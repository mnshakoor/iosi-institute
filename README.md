IOSI Institute is the planned learning and resource gateway for the IOSI Global community, its members, institutional stakeholders, and strategic partners. The platform will provide structured access to public learning materials, selected partner resources, practical analytic tools, and standards-aligned guidance produced or managed by ARAC International Inc. and Lladner Business Solutions through Quanta Analytica and MNS Consulting.

The Institute is designed to help analysts, risk managers, humanitarian practitioners, researchers, security professionals, students, and organizational leaders make better decisions under uncertainty.

## Mission

IOSI Institute will translate rigorous analytic methods into practical, reproducible learning resources. Its purpose is to:

- strengthen analytic judgment, evidence discipline, and decision quality;
- teach Structured Analytic Techniques for complex and uncertain environments;
- improve the collection, validation, and citation of web-sourced information;
- connect analytic standards with organizational risk-management practice;
- demonstrate responsible CARVER adaptation for strategic and operational prioritization;
- support security risk management across corporate, nonprofit, humanitarian, research, and institutional settings; and
- curate credible, freely available resources for global security, intelligence analysis, research, and risk management.

## Learning and Resource Pathways

### 1. Analytic Tradecraft

Foundational guidance for producing analysis that is objective, timely, transparent, evidence-based, and clear about uncertainty.

Planned resources include:

- problem definition and decision-customer analysis;
- key intelligence questions and collection requirements;
- fact, assumption, inference, and judgment separation;
- source evaluation and evidence traceability;
- confidence statements and estimative language;
- key judgments, indicators, warnings, and scenario design;
- peer review, challenge analysis, and lessons learned; and
- concise formats such as 5W1H situation reports and executive briefs.

### 2. Structured Analytic Techniques

Practical guides, templates, and worked examples for techniques that reduce bias, expose assumptions, test alternatives, and make reasoning auditable.

Initial technique families will include:

- **Diagnostic:** Key Assumptions Check, Analysis of Competing Hypotheses, quality-of-information review
- **Contrarian:** Devil's Advocacy, Red Team Analysis, What-If Analysis
- **Exploratory:** Alternative Futures, scenario analysis, indicators and signposts
- **Structuring:** actor mapping, causal-loop mapping, chronology, ranking, and matrix analysis
- **Reframing:** outside-in analysis, structured analogy, cognitive-linguistic and narrative analysis

### 3. WSI and ICD-Aligned Analytic Standards

Resources will explain how Web-Sourced Information (WSI) practices and Intelligence Community Directive 203 analytic principles can improve public-source analysis.

The learning pathway will emphasize:

- provenance and source-authority checks;
- independent corroboration and triangulation;
- claim-to-source traceability;
- citation completeness and clean URL practices;
- source limitations, information gaps, and deception risk;
- distinction between verified facts and analytic assessments;
- alternative analysis and explicit uncertainty; and
- reproducible evidence and judgment trails.

The platform will describe alignment accurately. Educational materials may be designed to support ICD 203-style analytic discipline without implying U.S. Intelligence Community certification, endorsement, or formal compliance.

### 4. CARVER for Organizational Decision-Making

The Institute will teach a rights-respecting adaptation of CARVER for prioritizing assets, processes, vulnerabilities, interventions, and resilience investments.

| Criterion | Decision question |
| --- | --- |
| Criticality | How essential is the asset, process, or function to the mission? |
| Accessibility | How exposed or reachable is it within the assessed environment? |
| Recuperability | How difficult and time-consuming would recovery be? |
| Vulnerability | How susceptible is it to disruption under current controls? |
| Effect | What direct and cascading consequences could follow? |
| Recognizability | How easily can the asset, weakness, or intervention point be identified? |

In the Quanta Analytica adaptation, higher recuperability scores indicate worse recovery conditions or a lower likelihood of rapid recovery. All scoring guidance must define its direction, scale, assumptions, and evidence requirements.

CARVER will be presented as a complementary prioritization method, not a replacement for enterprise or humanitarian security risk management. Applied modules will connect CARVER outputs to:

- ISO 31000 context, risk identification, analysis, evaluation, treatment, monitoring, and communication;
- GISF-aligned security risk management, duty of care, acceptance, protection, deterrence, and operational continuity;
- strategic portfolio decisions and resource allocation;
- operational planning, asset protection, business continuity, and program resilience; and
- ethical review, human-rights safeguards, and proportionality checks.

### 5. Global Security and Risk Resource Reviews

The Institute will curate and review freely available resources across:

- geopolitical and political-risk analysis;
- conflict systems, fragility, governance, and peacebuilding;
- humanitarian security risk management;
- OSINT methods, verification, and research design;
- information integrity, disinformation, and cognitive security;
- insider threat and organizational resilience;
- artificial-intelligence governance and model risk;
- business continuity and enterprise risk management; and
- strategic communication and counter-malign-influence analysis.

Each review should explain the resource's purpose, intended audience, strengths, limitations, level of difficulty, practical use cases, and access conditions. Inclusion does not constitute blanket endorsement.

## Content Principles

All Institute resources should be:

- **Decision-relevant:** connected to a real question, risk, or organizational choice
- **Method-transparent:** clear about assumptions, evidence, scoring, and reasoning
- **Source-disciplined:** traceable, corroborated where possible, and explicit about information gaps
- **Uncertainty-aware:** calibrated to the strength and consistency of the evidence
- **Rights-respecting:** consistent with privacy, human rights, proportionality, and do-no-harm principles
- **Operationally safe:** sanitized to protect sources, partners, affected communities, and sensitive methods
- **Accessible:** written in plain language, with acronyms defined and complex methods explained through examples
- **Adaptable:** suitable for multilingual, cross-sector, and regionally diverse audiences

## Publication and Repository Boundaries

This repository is intended to host the public landing-page code, approved public content, and metadata for linked resources. It should not become a storage location for restricted, licensed, proprietary, personal, or operationally sensitive material.

Do not commit:

- copyrighted books, journal issues, or commercial publications without redistribution rights;
- member-only or partner-controlled resources;
- contracts, statements of work, personal data, or internal contact information;
- operational security procedures that could create avoidable risk;
- raw source data containing sensitive identifiers; or
- assessments that have not completed analytic, legal, safeguarding, and publication review.

Where redistribution is not authorized, provide a lawful link, bibliographic record, original review, or short permitted excerpt instead of uploading the source file.

## Resource Review Lifecycle

1. **Nominate:** Identify a potentially useful public or partner resource.
2. **Verify:** Confirm authorship, provenance, access conditions, currency, and integrity.
3. **Assess:** Evaluate authority, analytic value, limitations, audience, and operational relevance.
4. **Safeguard:** Review copyright, privacy, sensitivity, and do-no-harm considerations.
5. **Approve:** Obtain the required analytic and editorial authorization.
6. **Publish:** Release the review with clear metadata and access conditions.
7. **Maintain:** Recheck links, versions, relevance, and risk on a defined schedule.
